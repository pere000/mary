Physical Time Travel is not possible to a non-existent spacetime

Markovianity Definition: Markovianity is the ontological condition whereby lawful state succession depends upon the presently actualized state and its presently applicable lawful transition structure, rather than upon an independently operative history.

P(St+1 | St, St−1, …, S0) = P(St+1 | St)

The equation above expresses the formal Markov condition. Markovianity itself is the ontological condition represented by that formal relation.

Present State

The present state is the only presently actualized space-time state.

S = actual state at t

St = {current organization, memory, current relations, transition conditions, …}

The present state contains actual structures through which non-present states may be represented or accessed without those states becoming presently actual.

Memory

Mt ⊂ St

Mt is a presently actualized memory structure within St.

The past does not remain ontologically operative as an independent state. Its presently actualized consequences may constitute structures within the present state.

More precisely:

St−1 is not presently actual.

Representation of St−1 within St may be presently actual.
Rep(St−1) ⊂ St

Memory therefore does not violate Markovianity. The past is represented within the present rather than remaining independently actual.

Admissible Successor States

The present state, together with its presently applicable lawful transition structure, constrains the space of lawfully admissible successor states.

St + Tt → {St+1(1), St+1(2), …}

Tt = presently applicable lawful transition structure

The successor states are admissible potentials, not presently actual states.

Markovianity does not require that only one successor state be possible. It characterizes the lawful dependence of successor-state admissibility upon the presently actualized state.

Markovianity is not equivalent to determinism.

Markovianity may characterize a state from which multiple successor states are lawfully admissible.

Determinism additionally concerns whether the lawful transition structure uniquely determines the successor state.

Relation to the Matrix
Markovianity does not replace or duplicate the ontological function of the Matrix. The Matrix admits lawful actualization.
Markovianity characterizes the lawful succession of presently actualized states
The Matrix concerns the admissibility and actualization of lawful possibilities.
Markovianity concerns the dependence of subsequent state succession upon the presently actualized state and its presently applicable lawful transition structure.
Markovianity therefore presupposes an already existing lawful ontological structure but does not constitute that structure itself.
Ontological and Formal Distinction
Markovianity is an ontological condition of lawful state succession.
Markov modeling is a formal representation of that succession.
Markovianity does not arise because a Markov model is constructed.
A Markov model represents a succession characterized by Markovian dependence and thereby provides a basis for conditional prediction of successor states.
Predictive success additionally depends upon whether the state representation, transition structure, and available information adequately characterize the system.
Markovianity therefore characterizes ontological state succession; Markov modeling formally represents that succession and provides a basis for its conditional prediction.
Principle of Non-Temporal Access
The present is the only presently actualized space-time state. It contains actual structures through which non-present past states may be represented or accessed, and it contains the presently actualized conditions defining the space of lawfully admissible successor states. By lawful action upon the present, an agent can alter its relation to representations of the past and alter which future states may subsequently become admissible or actualized, without temporal displacement. The past is accessible through the present; the future is alterable through the present; but neither requires the present to travel into another temporal state. History does not remain independently operative as past. Its presently actualized consequences may constitute structures within the present state. The future is not presently actual. It exists as a space of lawfully admissible successor possibilities constrained by the present state and its presently applicable transition structure.
