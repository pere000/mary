From Zigote to Human Being: Conception.

The embodied human individual is not constructed through learning but is ontologically constituted from human conception and progressively validated through lawful interaction with the environment. By virtue of its existence, the human individual necessarily exists as a hylomorphically actualized living neuropsychic physical organism. From conception, the intrinsic Aesthesis of Intelligence, acting through the Matrix and the Q-Form, establishes the organism's ontological individuation, unity, and persistence, thereby enabling the zygote to develop as one continuous living system. Thus, the neuropsychic operativity of the human organism precedes the progressive manifestation of Consciousness, providing the autopoietic foundation upon which consciously intentional participation subsequently becomes possible.

Neuropsychic development subsequently generates an operative animal World through the organism's ongoing structural coupling with its environment, in accordance with Maturana and Varela's theory of cognition. This initial World Construction neither presupposes Consciousness nor intentional semantic participation.

With the progressive manifestation of Consciousness—patterned through the human form—the objectifiable Intentional Subject (the Self) becomes constitutionally established as the unified center of a participation already intrinsically human, from which semantic interpretation, volition, and World Construction progressively unfold. The neuropsychically constituted world thereby progressively opens to intentional participation, semantic interpretation, validation, and refinement, enabling the continual refinement of increasingly sophisticated Worlds through the continuous recursive coupling between Consciousness and neuropsychic organization.

    The developmental constitution of the Intentional Subject provides a natural explanation for the progressive stabilization of bodily self-consciousness in human beings. Neuropsychic World Construction initially depends upon multisensory integration and structural coupling with the environment, making it susceptible to temporary perceptual misattributions such as the rubber hand illusion. As Consciousness progressively manifests and the objectifiable Intentional Subject (the Self) becomes constitutionally established, bodily ownership is no longer determined solely by neuropsychic sensorimotor integration but is increasingly refined through intentional participation, semantic interpretation, and recursive validation. Consequently, mature human subjects progressively acquire the capacity to recognize and correct such illusory constructions more effectively than non-human animals, whose operative Worlds remain predominantly organized through neuropsychic sensorimotor coupling.

    As reviewed by Ionta, Gassert, and Blanke (2011), the rubber hand illusion demonstrates that "by manipulating local aspects of body perception, it is possible to induce an illusory sense of ownership of a fake hand" (Botvinick & Cohen, 1998; Pavani et al., 2000; Ehrsson et al., 2004; Tsakiris & Haggard, 2005; Tsakiris et al., 2007; Aimola Davies et al., 2010). This finding indicates that bodily ownership depends, at least in part, upon the plasticity of multisensory and sensorimotor integration within the neuropsychic organization.

    The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that body schema and body image remain plastic. Within TACE, this plasticity permits the distinction of three ontological levels:

    Within TACE, this principle is not restricted to bodily ownership but is understood as a general characteristic of neuropsychic World Construction. The same neuropsychic mechanisms that permit temporary misattributions of bodily ownership are hypothesized to operate, with increasing semantic complexity, in the construction of progressively more abstract human Worlds, including social, symbolic, conceptual, and cultural realities. Conscious intentional participation progressively validates, refines, or corrects these constructions through recursive interaction with objective Reality.

    Consequently, TACE proposes that many forms of perceptual, conceptual, and symbolic distortion may be understood as neuropsychic misattributions occurring within progressively more abstract levels of World Construction rather than as alterations of the ontological identity of the human person. This proposal extends the empirical findings of multisensory body-ownership research into a broader ontological framework and should therefore be regarded as a theoretical hypothesis requiring further empirical investigation.

    Anticipatory perception does not by itself imply an emerging or implicit Consciousness. It may arise from the neuropsychic organism's capacity to infer and regulate toward probable future states on the basis of its present organization, prior interaction, and environmental signals. Such anticipation is therefore an operational form of cognition, not necessarily a conscious representation of the future. Therefore, from the TACE perspective, assertions that directly infer anticipatory perceptual awareness from such evidence are not justified by the evidence alone. For example: “In sum, evidence indicates an anticipatory perceptual awareness present in foetuses first in their prospectively organised bodily actions, then in sensory responses to their environment.” (Jonathan Delafield-Butt and Anna Ciaunica (2024), “Sensorimotor foundations of self-consciousness in utero”, Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences, 59, 101428.)

    Anticipatory perception in the developing fetus is sometimes interpreted as evidence of emerging consciousness or prereflective self-consciousness. Delafield-Butt and Ciaunica (2024) indeed interpret fetal anticipatory sensorimotor organization as evidence relevant to the emergence of bodily self-consciousness. However, at the TACE level, the observed anticipatory behavior can be described more minimally as neuropsychic prospective organization: the organism infers and anticipates probable sensory consequences of its actions without this, by itself, constituting intentional Consciousness or requiring a consciously represented future. The distinction is essential because anticipatory cognition and regulation can precede—and need not entail—intentional Consciousness.

    However, prereflective self-awareness, insofar as it reflects increasingly organized bodily self-related processes, may indicate the emergence of the biological substrate necessary for human organisms to become capable of the actualization of Consciousness. This distinction is theologically relevant from a Catholic perspective because it permits the biological development of the organism and the emergence of the physical capacity for conscious activity to be distinguished from the ontological constitution of the human person. TACE therefore does not identify the emergence of Consciousness with the generation of the soul, nor does it reduce the soul to neuropsychic organization.

    The human soul is not a biological outcome of embryonic development; it is created by God and constitutes the human person in hylomorphic union with the developing human organism from conception.
