Markovianity, Time, and Non-Temporal Access

Lawful State Succession, Historical Construction, and the Possibility of Retrospective Intervention

What does Markovianity imply about the past and the future? Within TACE, this question cannot be answered by treating time merely as a coordinate through which an observer moves. It requires a distinction between the presently actualized state, prior actualized states, and future admissible potentialities.

The central formal relation is the Markov condition:

P(St+1 | St, St-1, …, S0) = P(St+1 | St)

Within TACE, Markovianity is the ontological condition whereby lawful state succession depends upon the presently actualized state and its presently applicable lawful transition structure, rather than upon an independently operative history.

The equation itself is a formal Markov condition. It should not be treated as a complete metaphysical theory of time. Its ontological significance within TACE arises from its interpretation together with actualization, potentiality, and historical succession.

1. Markovianity and Temporal Displacement

The Markov condition does not by itself prove that the past has been "overwritten," nor does it by itself prove that time travel is impossible. Those claims require additional ontological premises.

TACE supplies such premises through its distinction between actualization, potentiality, and historical succession. A prior state has been actualized, but it is no longer the presently actualized state. A future state has not yet been actualized, although admissible successor states may already be constrained by the present.

St-1  →  St  →  {St+1(1), St+1(2), …}

Temporal succession is therefore not necessarily a sequence of simultaneously existing temporal locations through which an observer travels. Within this TACE interpretation, it is a succession of actualized states.

The past may be represented in the present without remaining independently actualized in the present.

2. The Past and Its Operative Presence in the Present

A prior actualization can remain operative in the present, but not as an independently actualized past state. What persists is not the past state itself as a second present, but a presently actualized constitution, consequence, or determination originating through prior actualization.

Thus the relation should not be reduced to a mere representation of the past. Where a prior actualization genuinely determines or constitutes something presently actual, that determination is operative in the present.

Past state → Operative constitution of present state

This is compatible with Markovianity. Once the operative consequence or constitution is presently actualized within St, subsequent lawful succession depends upon St and its applicable transition structure, rather than requiring the independently operative presence of St-1.

P(St+1 | St, St-1, …) = P(St+1 | St)

This must be distinguished from a record and from a mental phantom. A record is a real present entity—a physical inscription, photograph, database entry, or other presently existing structure—that records or refers to something past. Its present existence and possible causal efficacy belong to the present; it does not make the past itself presently operative.

A mental phantom is different. A subject's memory, image, or impression of a past event is a present mental or phenomenal event. It is a shadow of what was actual, not a record that preserves the past itself. Like the mental image of a unicorn, the phantom can be presently experienced even when its intentional object is not presently real.

Present record ≠ Past actuality
Present phantom ≠ Past actuality

A record is therefore a real present entity that refers to what was; a phantom is a present appearance whose intentional object need not presently exist. Neither makes its object presently actual.

This distinction is essential when a past offense has been forgiven. The offense can be erased while a record or psychological phantom of the offense remains for a time. Such persistence does not restore the offense or make it causally operative again.

The past does not persist as an independently actualized state. What may persist is a real present record or a present mental phantom referring to what was.

Accordingly, what may ordinarily be called the "influence of the past" must be distinguished from mere historical reference or psychological appearance. A genuinely operative consequence belongs to present reality; a record belongs to present reality as a record; a phantom belongs to present phenomenology as a phantom. None requires the past itself to remain causally operative.

3. The Future as Admissible Potential

The same distinction applies in the opposite direction. The future is not required to exist as a pre-constructed temporal landscape. Relative to the present, it consists of admissible potential successor states.

Their admissibility is constrained by the presently actualized state and the lawful transition structure applicable to it.

Unactualized does not mean unconstrained.

This also clarifies a frequent conceptual error: Markovianity does not imply determinism. A Markovian system may possess several admissible successor states. Markovianity specifies the dependency structure of succession; it does not, by itself, require a unique successor.

4. Human Agency and Historical Construction

Human agents already participate in the actualization of history. Every intentional act occurs within a presently actualized state and contributes to the lawful determination of subsequent states.

St → St+1

An intentional act can therefore alter which admissible successor state becomes actualized. In this sense, human beings do not merely inhabit history; they participate in its ongoing construction.

This does not violate Markovianity. The agent's act is itself part of the present configuration from which subsequent lawful succession proceeds.

The extraordinary question is different:

Could an agent alter the present in such a way that the present representation of an already actualized history were also transformed?

That would constitute a genuinely non-temporal form of historical intervention.

Markovianity alone does not establish whether such intervention is possible. It establishes only that, once a modified present state is actualized, subsequent lawful succession proceeds from that state.

This distinction gives us:

Historical construction

as an ordinary consequence of conscious participation in actualization, versus

Retrospective historical alteration

as the genuinely extraordinary problem that Non-Temporal Access would need to address.

5. Non-Temporal Access

The question of time travel therefore becomes more precise. The important question is not whether an agent can simply move into a past region of spacetime, but whether an agent could interact with historical structure without travelling to an independently existing past.

This motivates a TACE concept of Non-Temporal Access: a hypothetical mode of interaction with historical structure that does not require spatial or temporal displacement into a separately existing past.

Presently actualized state
↓
Lawful intervention, if ontologically permitted
↓
Modified presently actualized state
↓
Lawful successor states

The important point is that any such intervention would occur through the present state. The agent would not travel backward into a past region of spacetime.

Scope condition: Non-Temporal Access is a TACE hypothesis to be investigated. It is not a theorem derived from the Markov condition alone.

6. Historical Intervention: A Hypothetical Case

The distinction between ordinary human agency and the stronger time-travel hypothesis can now be stated precisely.

Historical construction
Present action → subsequent actualization

Retrospective historical alteration
Present intervention → transformation of the present representation of an already actualized history

The first is ordinary conscious participation in historical actualization. Every intentional action that contributes to a different lawful successor state participates in the construction of subsequent history.

The second concerns the possibility of altering presently actualized structures that refer to, derive from, or remain causally related to an already actualized history. The past event itself is not thereby acted upon as a presently operative entity; what changes is the present state through which a relation to that history is maintained.

The second question requires additional ontological principles beyond Markovianity. Markovianity establishes how subsequent succession depends upon the modified present; it does not establish whether such retrospective intervention is possible.

7. The Question of Historical Deletion

Suppose an agent possessed an ontological capacity to modify the presently actualized state in a way that altered structures constituting the present representation of a historical event.

At the level of Markovian succession, the consequence would be straightforward: the modified present would become the operative state from which subsequent succession proceeds.

St → S't      P(St+1 | S't)

No second timeline is required merely by the formal structure of Markovian succession. But Markovianity does not establish that such an intervention is physically or ontologically possible. That would require an independent account of the agent's capacity, scope, causal consistency, and identity preservation.

8. Markovianity, Selectivity, Determinism, and Actualization

The strongest version of the hypothesis would be the apparent deletion of a historical event. If an agent could lawfully modify every presently actualized structure through which an event remains represented or causally operative, the resulting present would no longer contain those structures.

This should not be described as travelling into the past and erasing an event. The hypothetical operation would instead modify the present configuration through which that history remains represented and operative.

Whether such complete intervention is possible remains an open ontological question. It requires an account of the intervention itself, the completeness of historical traces, identity preservation, causal consistency, and the relationship between the modified present and prior succession.

9. The TACE Architecture of State Succession

TACE should preserve a strict distinction among these concepts.

ConceptRole
MarkovianityCharacterizes lawful state succession and its dependence on the present state.
SelectivityConcerns lawful bifurcation and admissibility.
SelectionConcerns determination of an admissible successor.
DeterminismConcerns whether lawful conditions uniquely determine the successor.
ActualizationConcerns the passage from potential to actual state.

These concepts cooperate within one architecture but are not interchangeable. Markovianity should therefore not be treated as another actualizing principle alongside the Matrix.

Markovianity characterizes the flow of states; it does not become the ontological source of that flow.

11. Conclusion

TACE need not choose between a static block universe and an unconstrained presentism. It can instead describe reality as lawful historical succession.

The present is actual. The past is no longer actual as past state, although it may remain represented within the present. The future is not yet actual, although its admissible possibilities are constrained by the present and its lawful transition structure.

Actual Present
↓
Lawful Successor Potential
↓
Future Actualization

The deeper significance of Markovianity within TACE is therefore not that it proves a theory of time travel. Its significance is that it provides a formal characterization of lawful state succession.

Reality proceeds from what is presently actualized toward what is lawfully admissible for subsequent actualization.

The past does not have to remain a second world, and the future does not have to pre-exist as another world. There is one reality, continuously actualized through lawful succession.

12. Conclusion

The Markovian interpretation becomes most powerful when three levels are kept distinct.

LevelQuestion
FormalHow does the next state depend on the current and prior states?
OntologicalWhat is presently actual, what is no longer actual, and what remains potential?
SpeculativeCould an agent alter the present in a way that also transforms the present representation of an already actualized history?

The first is the mathematical Markov condition. The second is TACE's ontological interpretation of actualization and succession, including the distinction between a past event and a presently actualized relation to that event. The third is an application that requires further ontological investigation.

Keeping these levels separate prevents Markovianity from being asked to prove more than it actually establishes, while allowing it to occupy a precise and potentially important place within TACE.
