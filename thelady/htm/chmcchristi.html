Compassionate Heart of the Mystici Corporis Christi
Lyric & Prose


The Mystici Corporis Christi encyclical, written by Our Pope Pio XII, beautifully blends Paul's theology of the Church as a unified “Body” with Peter's teaching of the Church as a “Spiritual House” made of living stones. On the other hand, in our day, the biologists Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela formulated the theory that, through autopoiesis, a cell is capable of cognition simply by virtue of being alive (Santiago Theory of Cognition). However, Niklas Luhmann bridged both perspectives, taking the argument a step further by developing the theory of social systems as autopoietic systems capable of reproducing themselves through communication.
Communication possesses an inherent capacity to generate self-organization when environmental conditions favor the emergence of living systems. Within the Tegmark–Aquinas Conceptual Ecosystem (TACE), Intelligence is fundamental and omnipresent throughout Creation. Thus, TACE asks what makes lawful self-organization possible in the first place, identifying Intelligence as the overlooked ontological dimension underlying lawful organization and, consequently, Darwinian evolutionary processes.
Therefore, Christians can easily conceive the Mystici Corporis Christi as a living system capable of cognition. At least within the context of the Faith of Piety, they might furthermore proclaim, after millennia of historical survival under the Shadow of God, from Eden to Eden:

Mystici Corporis Christi, Gratia Plena, Dominus Tecum! – Hail!
It has never been the biblical pattern that God saves humanity by preserving the genetic lineage of a particular individual or heterosexual couple, as one might preserve a breeding line among plants or animals. Scripture presents God's relationship with humanity in fundamentally different terms: not as the preservation of a biological stock, but as the restoration and reconstitution of humanity itself.
History, by contrast, provides numerous examples in which religion and political power became intertwined with the preservation of particular bloodlines. Dynastic systems could sacralize hereditary succession, as occurred in various ancient civilizations, while modern racial ideologies radically transformed the notion of blood into an instrument of political exclusion and domination. Under National Socialism, the language of racial and hereditary purity was developed into a murderous state ideology. In Imperial Japan, a different form of sacred nationalism likewise connected political identity with an allegedly unbroken divine imperial lineage, particularly through the ideology surrounding the emperor and State Shinto.
The biblical pattern is fundamentally different. God has always willed Humanity as more than a mere collection of human individuals, and peoples as more than aggregates of persons. Whoever truly loves a people must therefore love its constituent persons; yet the reverse is not equivalent, because loving individual persons does not necessarily entail loving the people to which they belong as a corporate reality.
This is reflected in the apostolic contrast between Adam and Christ. As Paul writes: “For since by man came death, by man came also the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam (individual) all die (people), even so in Christ (individual) shall all be made alive (people)” — 1 Corinthians 15:21–22 The contrast is not between one genetic lineage and another, but between two representative human realities: Adam and Christ. Through one man, death enters into the human condition; through one Man, Jesus Christ, resurrection and life are given to humanity. Paul develops this same structure explicitly in Romans 5:12–19, where the disobedience of the one is set against the obedience of the One: “by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one shall many be made righteous”.
Thus, the biblical logic is not the preservation of a bloodline for the salvation of humanity, but the restoration of humanity through its representative Head. Adam represents fallen humanity; Christ represents redeemed humanity. The One does not merely preserve a biological lineage—the One reconstitutes the human order itself.
The Redeemer is not merely an individual but the embodiment and representative Head of a people. Just as Noah became the bearer of a renewed humanity through his household—his three sons, Shem, Ham, and Japheth—after the Flood, Christ is the divine Bearer and Head of the People of God, the universal Church.
He is invested with all authority, both cosmic and divine, and His priesthood is not an isolated innovation but the fulfillment of a priestly tradition already embodied in Melchizedek, priest and king of God Most High (El Elyon). Melchizedek therefore stands within the biblical genealogy of Christ's priesthood not as the servant of a merely Mesopotamian deity, but as a priest of the Most High God.
This is precisely the typological claim expressed in Psalm 110:4, where God swears concerning the Messiah: 
You are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek

Thus, Christ gathers into Himself what Noah prefigured: not merely the preservation of an individual biological lineage, but the preservation, renewal, and reconstitution of humanity as a people under God. His universality is therefore not genetic but covenantal, priestly, and ontological: the One through whom the many are gathered into a renewed humanity.
