TACE and Universal Consciousness

Structural proximity and ontological distance between two approaches to consciousness and reality
TACE consultation document · Comparative / methodological note · 2026-09-13
Structural resemblance · Fundamental difference · Matrix · Formalization · Sources

A universal-consciousness approach and TACE can appear surprisingly close when viewed at the level of their motivating questions. Both challenge a purely physicalist picture and seek a deeper structure underlying physical reality and conscious experience. Yet their foundational ontologies are substantially different.

Important methodological status. The universal-consciousness paper discussed here was retracted. It should therefore be treated as a comparative philosophical case study, not as scientific evidence for TACE. Its retraction is also useful as a methodological warning: an ontological proposal, mathematical notation, or proposed operator does not by itself constitute an empirically established physical theory.

1. The structural resemblance

The universal-consciousness proposal can be represented approximately as:

Ontological reading: The diagram does not represent Consciousness or Matrix as generated by Intelligence. The Realm of Forms is created, originally actualized from Prime Potency, rather than generated or engendered. Intelligence, Consciousness, and Matrix are presented as constituents of that created Realm. Participation is shown as the relational nexus, while Matrix rules admissible relations. G-Constructivity and lawful actualization distinguish the constitutional relations from the subsequent formal/actualization pathway.

The resemblance is therefore genuine. Both approaches ask how a deeper, non-naively-physical order can relate to differentiated reality and individual experience.

  • Physical reality is not necessarily ontologically fundamental.
  • Mathematical or formal structure has a role prior to physical realization.
  • Differentiation requires an underlying principle of order.
  • Individual conscious experience must be related to a deeper ontological structure.
  • Consciousness cannot simply be identified with an ordinary physical description. Each conscious being is ontologically unique: each human soul is unique. Your feelings are not transferable or numerically reproducible in another being—not even in an angel.

2. The decisive difference: what is fundamental?

The universal-consciousness approach places Consciousness at the foundation of reality. TACE does not.

TACE places Intelligence prior to Consciousness. Consciousness is irreducible, but it is not treated as the universal substrate from which everything else is generated.

Universal intelligence does not imply universal consciousness.

This distinction prevents TACE from collapsing Intelligence, Consciousness, and the lawful structure of actualization into a single universal field.

3. The Matrix as the major point of contact

The universal-consciousness proposal introduces a mechanism by which an undifferentiated foundational condition becomes differentiated reality. TACE approaches the same general problem by asking several separate questions:

  • What makes a possibility admissible?
  • What structures lawful differentiation?
  • What determines which transitions are lawful?
  • How does admissible formal possibility become historically actualized?

The Matrix is central to this architecture. It is not consciousness, a conscious observer, or an agent making choices. It is the ontological principle of lawful mathematical operativity and transformation. But TACE can ask whether the collapse of the wave function could—for some as-yet-unknown reason—be synchronous with the perception of consciousness./p>

Within the TACE architecture, Selectivity is the formal principle defining lawful bifurcations, while Selection belongs to the Matrix. This distinction separates the structure of admissible alternatives from the actual historical transition among them.

4. The crucial unresolved issue: mathematical formalization of the Matrix

TACE should not conceal an important limitation of its present development: the Matrix has not yet been mathematically formalized to the point at which its proposed dynamics can be empirically tested.

The TACE architecture is considerably more explicit at the ontological and constitutional level than it is at the level of a completed mathematical theory. The mathematical formalization necessary to derive quantitative, falsifiable predictions from the Matrix, once sufficiently developed, remains future work.

In this respect, TACE may offer Dr. Maria Strømme and others a broader ontological framework within which the problem of falsifiable prediction could be reformulated and potentially resolved, provided that the Matrix can be developed into a mathematically explicit and empirically testable theory.

This marks the boundary between:

ontological architecture
        ↓
formal mathematical specification
        ↓
physical realization
        ↓
quantitative prediction
        ↓
experimental test

A central future question for TACE is therefore:

Can the ontological architecture of the Matrix be translated into mathematics sufficiently precise to generate predictions that nature can distinguish from competing theories?

TACE does not assert that the ultimate substrate of reality is necessarily intelligible. It identifies Intelligence as the intelligible ultimate substrate—the ultimate ground of intelligibility—while leaving open whether there exists a deeper substrate that is intrinsically non-intelligible to any mode of being.

6. The deeper relationship

The universal-consciousness approach asks whether Consciousness can be the foundation of reality. TACE asks whether Consciousness itself can be situated within a deeper architecture of Intelligence, lawful operativity, mathematical structure, actualization, and meaning.

This makes TACE neither simply a rejection nor a repetition of the universal-consciousness approach. It is a different ontological response to a closely related fundamental problem.

In TACE, Consciousness constitutes the highest range of individuality and power within the created Realm of Forms, while remaining ontologically served by Intelligence.

7. The methodological lesson for TACE

The retraction of the universal-consciousness paper provides a particularly relevant methodological lesson for TACE.

A beautiful ontology is not yet a physical theory.

A mathematical symbol is not yet a measurable physical quantity.

A proposed operator is not yet an experimentally established mechanism.

TACE should apply the same standard to itself. Its primary objective is to remain open to rational and scientific coherence. It is not one of its objectives to claim experimental confirmation before the relevant mathematics exists. However, the next major step is to make the Matrix mathematically precise and then determine whether that formalization produces predictions that can be tested against nature.
