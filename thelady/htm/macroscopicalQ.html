1. Cognitive organization and consciousness

As Andrew Gallimore argues, brains construct their own world models. However, according to the Santiago Theory of Cognition, cognition does not require a brain: living organisms generate and maintain their own domains of interaction with the environment through their biological organization. Therefore, an alien, whether possessing a brain or not, can be endowed with cognitive properties. Consciousness is a much more complicated issue; the two concepts must not be conflated.

Within the ontological position articulated in the Diaspora post, Aliens and DMT Worlds: Minds & the Quantum Field, all biological beings, including plants, non-human animals, and hypothetical extraterrestrial life forms, cannot undergo hallucinations, because hallucination presupposes conscious experience. They possess cognitive properties according to their respective modes of biological organization; however, cognitive organization does not entail consciousness. Consequently, regardless of whether a non-conscious organism metabolizes DMT or other psychoactive compounds, it cannot experience hallucinations.

2. The proposed interaction hypothesis

The hypothesis begins with the following challenge:

Any being capable of observing quantum patterns and coupling with the observed target theoretically could interact with it beyond its immediate sensory system, and consciousness if endowed with it.

The question is whether physical interactions between distinct entities might involve their underlying quantum patterns rather than being restricted to their immediately observable macroscopic configurations. Yet, in a reality that manifests prior to its apprehension by senses or scientific instruments as a continuously flowing Heraclitean reality, the macroscopic cannot be regarded as disconnected from its quantum constitution. A macroscopic entity is not an independent reality standing apart from its quantum organization; it is the presently actualized macroscopic manifestation of that organization. Consequently, physical interaction between entities must be considered in relation to the quantum configurations and relations constitutive of their physical existence, even when those interactions are not directly accessible to ordinary perception or measurement.

3. The Quantum Field–Matrix Operative Unit

Within the proposed framework, a canonical proposition provides the starting point:

Quantum Field (Physical Realm) + Matrix (Non-Physical Realm) = Operative Unit

The Quantum Field and the Matrix constitute a single operative unit in which the physical and formal dimensions are jointly organized.

  1. Quantum Field: Provides the physical domain of quantum configurations and their possible couplings.
  2. Matrix: Governs their formal admissibility, compatibility, and lawful transitions.

This proposition suggests a theoretical possibility worth investigating: physical interaction between distinct entities might not be restricted to their immediately observable macroscopic configurations. Their underlying quantum patterns could participate in couplings governed by the Operative Unit.

Physical coupling belongs to the Operative Unit; however, it is the Matrix which establishes the lawfulness governing that coupling.

The sequence describes the relationship between two macroscopic entities through their quantum-field configurations and the lawful coupling of their quantum patterns.

The theoretical framework provides an ontological interpretation of the sequence. 5. Could this explain reported encounter after-effects?

The source text observes that reports of encounters between human beings and alleged non-human intelligences sometimes describe phenomena that are not, in every respect, mediated by ordinary sensory interaction. It also notes that the proportion of cases, whether published or unpublished, remains unknown.

Some reports describe alleged consequences extending beyond the immediate experience, including:

  • Physiological effects on the human organism.
  • Changes in the surrounding physical environment.
  • Reported interference with electronic equipment.
  • Alterations in sensory perception.
  • Persistent psychological or experiential consequences.
  • Reported communications occurring against the person's will, described as though intrusive entities had taken possession of their minds.

The hypothesis proposes that at least some such reports could be investigated as possible consequences of physical interactions involving quantum configurations, rather than assuming that every reported phenomenon must originate in ordinary sensory stimulation.

6. Three distinct domains of interaction

The framework distinguishes three domains:

(I) Physical domain: The Quantum Field–Matrix Operative Unit governs the admissibility and actualization of physical interactions.

(II) Domain of Perception (Interface Hypothesis of Perception - D. Hoffman): Human neuropsychic organization processes environmental information and determines what becomes perceptually accessible.

(III) Domain of Experience (Restructuring consciousness –the psychedelic state in light of integrated information theory - A. Gallimore): The conscious human being apprehends and rationally evaluates the existential significance of what is experienced.

These domains must not be conflated. A physical interaction need not be directly perceived, and a reported conscious experience does not, by itself, establish that a corresponding external physical interaction occurred.

7. The central hypothesis

An entity capable of accessing relevant quantum patterns and establishing a compatible coupling through the Quantum Field–Matrix Operative Unit could, in principle, interact physically with another entity beyond the latter's immediate sensory accessibility. Where the target is a human being, such interaction could potentially produce physical or environmental changes that subsequently become available to perception and conscious experience.

This would provide a conceptual framework for investigating some reported human–non-human intelligence encounters and their alleged after-effects.

8. Human consciousness and perceptual access

Human beings are endowed with consciousness; yet, generally, they do not attempt to examine what they could access through this capacity. They may remain unaware of the icons that, through their perceptual interfaces, could allow them to distinguish between entities that exist independently of their own consciousness and those that are merely puppets automatically generated by their brains and experienced without sufficient conscious involvement through will, reason, and instruction.

10. Scientific qualification

This document presents an exploratory ontological hypothesis from the TACE framework. The proposed role of the Matrix as a non-physical principle governing the lawfulness of quantum-pattern coupling is not established by standard quantum field theory.

The conceptual distinctions among physical interaction, perceptual access, and conscious experience remain important even when the proposed physical explanation has not been established.
