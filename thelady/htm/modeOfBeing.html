A central distinction in Tace concerns the relationship between consciousness and intelligence. Consciousness essentially involves choosing a target (Lat. attendere, "to intentionally direct one's mind toward"). According to Tace, this capacity is unique to conscious beings. Animals do not intentionally choose or focus on a target; rather, their focus is directed deterministically by instinct, conditioning, and reactions to stimuli. Accordingly, Tace regards intelligence as an attribute of consciousness rather than consciousness as an emergent property of intelligence. Consciousness is therefore ontologically prior to intelligence, not reducible to increasingly sophisticated information processing. For this reason, Tace rejects theories that identify consciousness with computational or informational complexity. Intelligence and consciousness are related, but they are not equivalent concepts; nor should intelligent agency be taken as sufficient evidence of consciousness.
