# TACE Theology: An Introduction ### The Ontological Foundation for a Dialogue Between the Logos and Creatures The possibility of a dialogue between the Logos and creatures raises a fundamental theological question: how can beings possessing distinct modes of existence participate in a reality in which truth, meaning, and intelligibility are shared? Within the Tegmark–Aquinas Conceptual Ecosystem (TACE), this question emerges from the relationship between ontology, consciousness, and the possibility of a shared world of intelligibility. TACE distinguishes the **Physical Realm** from the **Realm of Forms**, while placing God, the Necessary Being and Unmoved Mover, beyond the Reality of Forms. Consciousness is not reduced to biological organization or physical processes; it belongs to an ontological order that transcends the Physical Realm. This distinction opens the inquiry into how creatures can apprehend reality according to their respective modes of being without requiring identical forms of perception or experience. The concept of a **consensus cosmic world** provides a further point of departure. Different conscious beings may possess distinct perceptual interfaces while participating in lawful relations through which entities and their significance become intelligible. Shared intelligibility does not require identical experiences; it requires that differences in apprehension need not prevent meaningful relations. This possibility leads toward the theological question of the **Logos**. In Christian theology, the Logos is not merely an abstract principle of communication or a universal structure of meaning. The Prologue of John identifies the Logos as being with God and as God, and declares that the Logos became flesh (John 1:1, 14). The Logos therefore belongs to the heart of Christian theology: the relationship between God and creation, divine intelligibility, and the possibility of communion between the Creator and creatures. TACE Theology begins its inquiry at the intersection of these questions. If creatures possess distinct modes of being, and if their apprehension of reality is conditioned by those modes, how can divine truth be communicated to them? What makes creatures capable of receiving, recognizing, and responding to the Logos? How should the relationship between created intelligibility and divine revelation be understood? The onto-epistemological interface of consciousness provides a conceptual starting point for investigating these questions. It helps distinguish the existence of a creature from the way reality becomes intelligible to that creature. It also opens a path toward examining whether a shared world of intelligibility can provide a basis for dialogue without reducing the Creator to creation or treating divine revelation as merely another perceptual interface. **TACE Theology proposes to investigate the relationship between the Logos and creatures through the distinctions among being, consciousness, intelligibility, and revelation.** Its purpose is to explore how creatures with different modes of being may stand in relation to divine truth while preserving the ontological distinction between the Necessary Being and created beings. This is an introduction to the inquiry, not its conclusion. The central task is to clarify how the metaphysical conditions of creaturely existence and intelligibility relate to the Christian understanding of the Logos, without confusing a philosophical account of communication with the mystery of divine self-revelation. *The dialogue between the Logos and creatures begins, therefore, not with the assumption that all beings apprehend reality in the same way, but with the question of how divine truth can address creatures according to their own modes of being.*
