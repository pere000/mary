Reality is a lawful actualization of admissible possibilities, not an ontological lottery.

In TACE, a recurrent ontological pattern appears throughout Reality:
Possibility → Admissibility → Act.

That is:
  • Possibility denotes what can be conceived or exist in itself.
  • Admissibility denotes the lawful delimitation of possibility such that it can participate in a determinate order of realization.
  • Act denotes the actualization of an admissible possibility.
    • For example, if the creation of Reality ex nihilo is admitted:
    Undetermined possibility → Unmoved Mover → lawful admissibility

    Thus, the notion of undetermined possibility already presupposes at least the possibility of some actuality. If “nothingness” is conceived as the absolute absence of any determinate existence, then nothingness cannot itself provide the ontological principle of actualization. The transition from nothing to something therefore cannot be explained by an intrinsic potency of nothingness. Creation ex nihilo requires an external and transcendent cause whose actuality and causal power are not conditioned by the possibilities of the created order. In TACE, this ultimate ground of actuality is identified with the Unmoved Mover of Aristotle, understood in the Thomistic tradition as Actus Purus.

    Therefore, this ontological progression can be formalized in three stages:
    1. Primordial Potency → Admissibility → Act (ex nihilo)
  • Primordial Potency denotes possibility in itself. Metaphysical actuality, grounded in the Unmoved Mover / Actus Purus, establishes the lawful admissibility through which Creation can exist rather than remaining undetermined possibility.
    • 2. Admissibility → Prime Potency → Design (Order)
  • Admissible possibility becomes Prime Potency: mathematical possibility formally delimited and therefore capable of determinate realization. At this stage, the lawful structure of what can be realized is determined as Design (Order).
    • 3. Prime Potency → Matrix → Actualization (Reality)
  • Prime Potency is operationally mediated by the Matrix, the Universal Operator, through which admissible formal possibilities are actualized as determinate Reality.

    • In TACE, anything that can be actualized is either an admissible potentiality or nothing at all.
    Drawing on the Aristotelian-Thomistic framework of hylomorphism, TACE distinguishes between two senses of potency. First, potency may be understood in a primordial sense as mere possibility: that which is formally conceivable or possible in itself. Second, potency may be understood as admissible possibility: a possibility that is compatible with the lawful organization of Reality and therefore capable of actualization.
    Thus, randomness may be conceivable in the primordial sense as an unrestricted possibility, but it does not thereby constitute an actualizable possibility. Actualization necessarily imposes a condition of admissibility: only possibilities permitted by the lawful organization of Reality can become actual. In TACE, this admissibility is determined by the Matrix.

    The empirical fact that we encounter one coherent Reality rather than an unrestricted plurality of simultaneously actualized alternatives is consistent with this distinction. We observe this Reality, not a plurality of independently actualized realities among which an unconstrained lottery has selected one. Alternative possibilities may be formally conceivable, but mutually incompatible alternatives cannot all be simultaneously actualized within the same lawful world order. TACE does not exclude the possibility that distinct actualizations could occur under other lawful world orders; however, the space of physically realizable Reality is necessarily constrained to a subset of the formally possible worlds. Where there is law, there is order: lawful admissibility necessarily delimits and orders the field of possible actualizations.

    Actualization therefore entails selection by admissibility and viability: from the field of primordial possibilities, only a coherent subset can be actualized as one historically continuous Reality within the universal space of possible worlds. In TACE, the Matrix determines this admissibility, while incompatible alternatives remain non-actualized possibilities rather than coexisting physical realities. This distinction grounds the TACE differentiation between Primordial Potency and Prime Potency: Primordial Potency denotes mere possibility as such, whereas Prime Potency denotes possibility that has been delimited by the lawful conditions of admissibility and is therefore capable of actualization.

    In Summary:
    TACE does not infer ontological randomness from epistemic probability. Instead, it proceeds from the ontological primacy of law-like admissibility and asks whether any observed probability distribution requires fundamental indeterminacy. Accordingly, quantum uncertainty is not, by itself, evidence of ontological randomness. Local quantum uncertainties do not constitute independent ontological indeterminacies that accumulate into global randomness; their actualizations remain constrained by the lawful mathematical organization of Reality.
