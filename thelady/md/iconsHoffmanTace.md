Gallimore’s **Consensus Reality Space (CRS)** is a concept from his *world-space* model of consciousness. It is important to distinguish it from the claim that consensus reality is metaphysically fundamental.

### 1. The basic idea

Gallimore proposes that the brain is a **world-building system**. At any particular moment, a particular large-scale configuration of brain activity corresponds to a particular experienced **World**.

The **World Space** is the conceptual state-space containing the possible phenomenal worlds that the brain can construct. A World is therefore not simply a physical environment; it is the complete phenomenal configuration experienced at a moment. ([Leftypol][1])

Within this enormous World Space, ordinary waking consciousness occupies a relatively restricted region:

**World Space**
→ **Consensus Reality Space**
→ individual waking-world states

Gallimore describes the Consensus Reality Space as something like an **attractor basin**: normal brain connectivity and sensory coupling naturally keep the brain moving through this particular region. ([PodScripts][2])

### 2. Why "consensus"?

"Consensus" does **not** mean that everybody literally experiences an identical phenomenal world.

Rather, normal human brains have evolved sufficiently similar architectures and are continuously coupled to the same external environment. Consequently, their constructed world-models exhibit a large degree of **structural agreement**.

For example, we can normally agree that there is:

* a three-dimensional spatial environment,
* persistent objects,
* other people,
* bodies,
* causal interactions,
* relatively stable temporal succession,
* colors, sounds, textures, etc.

But Gallimore explicitly recognizes that your constructed world and mine cannot simply be assumed to be phenomenally identical. Each brain constructs its own model. ([PodScripts][3])

So:

> **Consensus reality = a region of World Space characterized by the stable, mutually compatible world-models normally constructed by human brains.**

### 3. What DMT does in this model

This is where the concept becomes particularly interesting.

Gallimore's proposal is that psychedelics—especially DMT—**perturb the dynamics of the brain's world-building system**.

Normally:

**brain dynamics → CRS → ordinary waking World**

Under DMT:

**brain dynamics → leaves CRS → another region of World Space → DMT World**

He describes DMT as effectively pushing the system out of the attractor basin occupied by ordinary waking reality and into another region of the World Space. ([PodScripts][2])

Importantly, this does **not necessarily mean that the brain physically transports the person somewhere else**. Gallimore has repeatedly emphasized that even if a DMT world corresponds to something external or ontologically independent, the experienced world still has to be represented by the brain. ([Singju Post][4])

### 4. The crucial distinction

There are therefore three different things that should not be conflated:

| Concept                     | Gallimore's meaning                                                             |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Noumenal reality**        | Whatever exists independently of our phenomenal model                           |
| **World Space**             | Space of possible phenomenal worlds the brain can construct                     |
| **Consensus Reality Space** | Restricted region of World Space corresponding to ordinary waking human reality |

This distinction is particularly important for your TACE work.

Gallimore's CRS is **not equivalent to "physical reality itself."** In fact, his earlier formulation explicitly calls consensus reality a **functional phenomenal model** evolved for survival in the noumenal world. ([ALIENINSECT.NET][5])

That makes CRS much closer to a **phenomenological/epistemic layer** than to an ontological foundation.

### 5. Relation to the newer Gallimore–Hoffman model

This becomes even more explicit in Gallimore's recent collaboration with Donald Hoffman. Their 2026 framework treats perception as an interface and investigates whether DMT might alter the interface sufficiently to make normally inaccessible conscious agents perceptible. The published abstract describes the underlying alternative as a reality consisting of interacting conscious agents, with perception functioning as a species-specific interface rather than a veridical representation of an objective world. ([Sciety][6])

So the conceptual progression is roughly:

**Gallimore's earlier model**

`Noumenal world → brain → Consensus Reality Space → phenomenal world`

then:

**World Space**

`possible phenomenal worlds`

with CRS being one restricted basin within it.

And in the newer Hoffman/Gallimore framework:

`conscious-agent reality → perceptual interface → experienced world`

with DMT potentially altering the interface and therefore changing the region of experiential space that becomes accessible. ([Trace Institute][7])
