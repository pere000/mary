Neuropsychic Consensus Reality and Conscious Consensus Reality

The concept of Consensus Reality, as employed by Andrew Gallimore, refers primarily to the human mode of being endowed with consciousness. It describes the shared waking reality constituted through the evolutionarily shaped neuropsychic organization of the human brain, through which environmental information becomes available for conscious experience. However, the underlying principle of species-specific cognitive organization need not be restricted to conscious beings.



Living organisms, including plants and non-human animals, exhibit forms of cognitive organization through which environmental information is selectively processed and organism–environment relations are regulated. These processes can operate without presupposing conscious experience. Moreover, cognitive collectives and coordinated biological organization can produce selective outcomes that contribute to species fitness and adaptive continuity. Consequently, the organization of environmental information is not exclusive to conscious human beings.

This broader consideration requires distinguishing two related concepts:

1. Neuropsychic Consensus Reality


It refers to the species-relative organization of environmental information through the interactions of their perceptual interfaces or organism's neuropsychic organization. It encompasses the selective processing of environmental information and the resulting organization of organism–environment relations. It does not necessarily require consciousness, intentional agreement, or a collectively symbolic world, it is suffcient a shared signal structures. It provides the broader conceptual basis for considering species-specific cognitive realities in plants and non-human animals, without attributing conscious experience to them.

2. Conscious Consensus Reality
It refers to the world out there consciously experienced, after being apprehended, interpreted, and collectively maintained by beings endowed with consciousness. It includes an individual conscious experience of shared meanings of a common construction of a collectively intelligible world. In this sense, Gallimore's account of human consensus reality represents a particular conscious manifestation of a more general principle of species-relative environmental organization.

Relationship between the concepts
Neuropsychic Consensus Reality constitutes the broader level of species-relative cognitive organization. In beings endowed with consciousness, this organization also provides conditions for the formation of Conscious Consensus Reality. The two concepts are therefore related but not interchangeable: neuropsychic organization does not necessarily entail consciousness, whereas conscious consensus reality necessarily presupposes conscious subjects.

Consciousness establishes the immediate evidence of its own existence, but the truthfulness of its apprehension of everything beyond that immediate evidence requires separate epistemological consideration. Thus, the epistemological integrity of Conscious Consensus Reality depends not only on the existence of consciousness, but also on the reliability of the perceptual and cognitive conditions through which its contents become available to reason.
